SECTION 1: ROTATING EQUIPMENT HAZARDS
---------------------------------------
1.1 Types of Rotating Equipment:
- Centrifugal pumps, compressors, turbines
- Agitators, mixers, fans and blowers
- Conveyors, extruders, and gear boxes

1.2 Main Hazards:
- Entanglement with rotating parts (coupling, shaft, impeller)
- Ejection of parts due to mechanical failure
- High temperature surfaces causing burns
- High pressure fluid release causing injection injuries
- Noise induced hearing loss (>85 dB requires ear protection)
- Vibration induced white finger syndrome (long-term exposure)

1.3 Required PPE for Rotating Equipment Work:
- Safety glasses (mandatory within 3 meters of operating equipment)
- Hearing protection (mandatory above 85 dB(A))
- Safety gloves (chemical-resistant for process chemicals)
- Safety boots (steel-toed, anti-slip)
- Face shield for high-pressure work
- Flame-retardant coverall (FRC) in hydrocarbon areas

SECTION 2: PERMIT TO WORK SYSTEM
----------------------------------
2.1 Types of Permits Required:
- Cold Work Permit: For all non-ignition hazard maintenance
- Hot Work Permit: For welding, grinding, cutting in process areas
- Confined Space Entry Permit: For work inside vessels, tanks, sumps
- Electrical Isolation Permit: For work on electrical equipment
- Height Work Permit: For work at elevation above 2 meters

2.2 Permit to Work Procedure:
Step 1: Identify work scope and hazards
Step 2: Isolate all energy sources (LOTO)
Step 3: Test atmosphere (if confined space or hydrocarbon area)
Step 4: Obtain permit from area supervisor
Step 5: Briefing of all team members
Step 6: Carry out work within permit validity
Step 7: Clear site, remove tools and equipment
Step 8: Close permit and verify safe restoration

SECTION 3: LOCKOUT TAGOUT (LOTO) PROCEDURE
--------------------------------------------
3.1 Purpose of LOTO:
LOTO prevents unexpected energization or startup of machinery during maintenance.

3.2 Energy Types to be Isolated:
- Electrical energy: Open and lock motor circuit breaker
- Hydraulic energy: Close and lock hydraulic supply valve, bleed pressure
- Pneumatic energy: Close and lock air supply, bleed lines to zero
- Gravity/potential energy: Block elevated parts, lower loads
- Thermal energy: Cool equipment to safe temperature, depressurize

3.3 LOTO Steps:
Step 1: Notify all affected employees
Step 2: Identify all energy sources
Step 3: Shut down equipment using normal stopping procedure
Step 4: Isolate all energy sources (close valves, open breakers)
Step 5: Apply personal lock and danger tag to each isolation point
Step 6: Release/restrain stored energy (bleed pressure, drain fluid)
Step 7: Verify isolation by attempting normal startup (NEVER start)
Step 8: Verify zero energy with test equipment (multimeter, pressure gauge)

SECTION 4: HAZARDOUS ENERGY CONTROL
--------------------------------------
4.1 Electrical Safety:
- All electrical work must be done by qualified electricians
- Minimum safe approach distance from energized conductors: 300mm (480V)
- Voltage must be tested before working on any electrical component
- Use insulated tools only when working near live parts

4.2 Pressure Systems Safety:
- Never open flanged connections under pressure
- Verify gauge reading shows zero before opening any connection
- Use appropriate rated pressure fittings and tubing
- Pressure test after reassembly before returning to service

4.3 Chemical Hazards:
- Refer to Material Safety Data Sheet (MSDS) for each chemical
- Use appropriate chemical-resistant PPE
- Have eyewash station available within 10 seconds travel time
- Decontamination shower required in acid/caustic areas

SECTION 5: EMERGENCY PROCEDURES
----------------------------------
5.1 Fire Emergency:
Step 1: Activate nearest fire alarm pull station
Step 2: Call plant emergency number (ext. 911)
Step 3: Use fire extinguisher ONLY if fire is small and you are trained
Step 4: Evacuate to designated muster point
Step 5: Do NOT re-enter building until cleared by emergency coordinator

5.2 Chemical Spill:
Step 1: Alert all persons in area
Step 2: If safe, stop source of spill
Step 3: Isolate area with barriers
Step 4: Don appropriate PPE before cleanup
Step 5: Report to supervisor and HSE team immediately

5.3 Medical Emergency:
Step 1: Call plant medical emergency number
Step 2: Do not move injured person unless in immediate danger
Step 3: Apply first aid if trained
Step 4: Keep injured person warm and still
Step 5: Accompany to medical facility

SECTION 6: INCIDENT REPORTING
--------------------------------
6.1 What Must Be Reported:
- All injuries (no matter how minor)
- Near misses and unsafe conditions
- Equipment damage or failure
- Environmental releases (any spill to ground or drain)
- Fire or explosion, even if extinguished immediately

6.2 Reporting Procedure:
- Immediate verbal report to supervisor
- Written incident report within 24 hours (use Form HSE-001)
- Root cause analysis for Lost Time Incidents (LTI)
- Safety alert distributed plant-wide for significant events

SECTION 7: VIBRATION AND NOISE MONITORING
-------------------------------------------
7.1 Vibration Standards (ISO 10816):
- Zone A: 0-2.8 mm/s RMS (New equipment, excellent condition)
- Zone B: 2.8-7.1 mm/s RMS (Acceptable for long-term operation)
- Zone C: 7.1-18 mm/s RMS (Unsatisfactory, causes alarm, plan maintenance)
- Zone D: above 18 mm/s RMS (Dangerous, stop equipment immediately)

7.2 Bearing Temperature Monitoring:
- Use calibrated IR thermometer or thermocouple probe
- Record readings in equipment log
- Compare with baseline readings taken when new
- Alert engineering if increase of more than 20°C from baseline

7.3 Noise Exposure Limits (AS/NZS 1269):
- 85 dB(A) TWA 8 hours: hearing protection required
- 140 dB peak: absolute limit regardless of duration
- Noise mapping required for areas above 85 dB(A)
